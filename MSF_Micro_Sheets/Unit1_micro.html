5Statement: If a,b∈Z, b>0, ∃ unique q,r: a=bq+r, 0≤r<b.
Proof: Multiples of b: 0,±b,±2b,…
Case(i) a multiple of b ⇒ a=bq, r=0.
Case(ii) else a lies between bq & b(q+1): bq<a<bq+b ⇒ 0<a−bq<b ⇒ r=a−bq, 0<r<b (existence).
Case(iii) Uniqueness: let a=bq+r=bq₁+r₁ ⇒ b(q−q₁)=r₁−r …(1) ⇒ b|(r₁−r); but |r₁−r|<b ⇒ r₁−r=0 ⇒ r₁=r. In (1): b(q−q₁)=0, b≠0 ⇒ q=q₁. Hence proved.

8Statement: Every integer n>1 can be written as a product of primes, uniquely except for order.
Existence: If n prime, done. If composite, n=a×b, 1<a,b<n; factor a,b further; process ends ⇒ product of primes.
Uniqueness: Suppose n=p₁p₂…p_k=q₁q₂…q_m. By Euclid's lemma (prime dividing a product divides a factor) p₁=some q_j; cancel. Repeat ⇒ same primes, same multiplicities, only order differs. Hence proved.
