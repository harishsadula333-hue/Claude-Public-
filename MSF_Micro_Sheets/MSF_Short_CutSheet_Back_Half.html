UI·10
Q. Write about L.C.M.
Ans: The Least Common Multiple (LCM) of two or more integers is the smallest positive integer that is exactly divisible by all the given integers.
Ex: multiples of 4=4,8,12,16,20…; multiples of 6=6,12,18,24… ∴ LCM(4,6)=12. Also gcd(a,b)·lcm(a,b)=a·b.
UI·8
Q. Define prime and Coprime numbers.
Ans: Prime numbers: natural numbers greater than 1 having exactly two positive divisors, 1 and itself. Ex: 2, 3, 5, 7.
Coprime numbers: two integers whose GCD is 1. Ex: 8 and 15, because gcd(8,15)=1.
UI·6
Q. Prove that the greatest common divisor is unique.
Ans: Assume d₁ and d₂ are both GCDs of a and b. Since d₁ is a GCD and d₂ is a common divisor, d₂|d₁. Similarly, since d₂ is a GCD, d₁|d₂. As both are positive integers, d₁|d₂ and d₂|d₁ ⇒ d₁=d₂. Therefore the GCD is unique. Hence proved.
UI·4
Q. State Fundamental theorem of arithmetic.
Ans: Every positive integer n>1 can be expressed as a product of primes uniquely (except for order), i.e. n=p₁^α₁·p₂^α₂·…·p_k^α_k where p₁<p₂<…<p_k are distinct primes dividing n. Ex: 60=2²×3×5.
UI·2
Q. The sum of two odd integers is an even integer.
Ans: Proof: Let a, b be two odd integers: a=2n₁+1, b=2n₂+1, n₁,n₂∈Z.
a+b=2n₁+1+2n₂+1=2n₁+2n₂+2=2(n₁+n₂+1)=2n₃ [n₃=n₁+n₂+1∈Z].
∴ a+b is an even integer.

UII·8
Q. Define Poisson distribution and give examples.
Ans: A random variable X has a Poisson distribution if it assumes only non-negative values and its pmf is P(X=x)=e^(−λ)λˣ/x!, x=0,1,2,…,∞, where λ=mean number of occurrences, x=number of occurrences, e=2.71828. Mean=Variance=λ.
Examples: 1) No. of phone calls received per hour. 2) No. of accidents per day. 3) No. of printing mistakes on a page.
UII·6
Q. Define binomial distribution and give examples.
Ans: A random variable X has a binomial distribution if it assumes only non-negative values and its probability mass function is P(X=x)=ⁿCₓ pˣ qⁿ⁻ˣ, x=0,1,…,n; p+q=1 (0 otherwise). Mean=np, Variance=npq.
Examples: 1) No. of defective bolts in a box of n bolts. 2) No. of post-graduates in a group of n men. 3) No. of heads in n coin tosses.
UII·4
Q. Write the properties of variance.
Ans: (i) Var(X)≥0
(ii) Var(c)=0, c is a constant
(iii) Var(X+c)=Var(X)
(iv) Var(cX)=c²Var(X)
(v) Var(X)=E(X²)−[E(X)]²
UII·2
Q. Define expected value and variance of a random variable.
Ans: Expected value: If X takes values x₁,x₂,…,xₙ with probabilities p₁,p₂,…,pₙ, the mathematical expectation (mean) is the sum of products of values and their probabilities: E(X)=Σx·p(x).
Variance: the expectation of [X−E(X)]²: V(X)=E[X−E(X)]²=E(X²)−[E(X)]², where E(X²)=Σx²p(x).
UIII·10
Q. Define sampling distribution of mean and proportion.
Ans: Sampling distribution of mean: the probability distribution of all possible sample means obtained from samples of a fixed size drawn from a population. μx̄=μ, SE(x̄)=σ/√n.
Sampling distribution of proportion: the probability distribution of all possible sample proportions obtained from samples of a fixed size. μp=P, SE(p)=√(PQ/n), Q=1−P.

UIII·6
Q. Define Central Limit Theorem.
Ans: When sufficiently large random samples are drawn from a population, the sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution, irrespective of the original population distribution, provided the population has a finite variance.
μx̄=μ, σx̄=σ/√n (n=sample size, μ=population mean, σ=population S.D.)
UIII·4
Q. A random sample of size 2 is drawn from the population 3, 7, 11, 12. If we consider the sampling distribution of the sample mean with replacement, find the sample mean.
Ans: Population mean μ=ΣX/N=(3+7+11+12)/4=33/4=8.25.
The mean of the sampling distribution of sample means equals the population mean. ∴ μx̄=8.25
UIII·2
Q. What is meant by statistic and parameter?
Ans: Statistic: a numerical value calculated from a sample; describes the characteristics of a sample. Ex: sample mean, sample variance.
Parameter: a numerical value that describes the characteristics of an entire population. Ex: population mean, population variance.
UIV·10
Q. Explain the terms Null hypothesis and Alternative hypothesis.
Ans: (Not in answer sheet; standard definitions)
Null hypothesis (H₀): a hypothesis of no difference, tested for possible rejection under the assumption that it is true. Ex: H₀: μ=μ₀.
Alternative hypothesis (H₁): any hypothesis complementary to H₀, accepted when H₀ is rejected. Ex: H₁: μ≠μ₀ (two-tailed), μ>μ₀ or μ<μ₀ (one-tailed).
UIV·8
Q. Write about the test statistic of difference of proportion.
Ans: H₀: P₁=P₂. z=(p₁−p₂)/S.E.(p₁−p₂).
(a) P₁,P₂ known: z=(p₁−p₂)/√(P₁Q₁/n₁+P₂Q₂/n₂).
(b) Unknown: (i) Substitution: z=(p₁−p₂)/√(p₁q₁/n₁+p₂q₂/n₂). (ii) Pooling: p=(n₁p₁+n₂p₂)/(n₁+n₂), q=1−p, z=(p₁−p₂)/√[pq(1/n₁+1/n₂)].
Reject H₀ if |z|>1.96 (5%), >2.58 (1%), >1.645 (10%).
UIV·6
Q. Write the procedure for Hypothesis testing.
Ans: 1) State the null hypothesis (H₀) and alternative hypothesis (H₁).
2) Choose the significance level (α), usually 0.05.
3) Select the suitable statistical test (z-test, t-test etc.).
4) Calculate the test statistic using sample data.
5) Find the critical value or p-value.
6) Make a decision: reject H₀ if |z|>critical value, else accept.
7) Write the conclusion in simple terms.
UIV·4
Q. What is a Type I error?
Ans: A Type I error occurs when the null hypothesis is rejected when it is actually true. Its probability is the level of significance α. (Type II error: accepting H₀ when it is false.)
UIV·2
Q. State interval of estimation.
Ans: Interval of estimation is a range of values used to estimate an unknown population parameter (like mean or proportion) instead of giving a single value. Ex: 95% CI for μ: (x̄−1.96σ/√n, x̄+1.96σ/√n).

Q. Write the relation between correlation and regression coefficients. Is it possible to have two variables x and y with regression coefficients 2.8 and −0.5? Explain.
Ans: Relation: r=±√(b_xy·b_yx) (r has the same sign as the regression coefficients).
With 2.8 and −0.5: r=√(2.8×(−0.5))=√(−1.4), not a real number (coefficients must have same sign and product ≤1). Thus it is not possible.

Q. Define least squares in curve fitting.
Ans: The method of least squares is a mathematical technique used in curve fitting to find the best fitting curve for a given set of data points by minimising the sum of squares of errors between actual values and estimated values: S=Σ(yᵢ−ŷᵢ)² is minimum.

Q. Karl Pearson's coefficient of correlation.
Ans: Karl Pearson suggested a method for measuring the magnitude of linear relationship between two variables (Product-Moment correlation coefficient), denoted r:
r=Cov(X,Y)/(σxσy)=Σ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ)/(nσxσy), σx=√[(1/n)Σ(xᵢ−x̄)²], σy=√[(1/n)Σ(yᵢ−ȳ)²]. −1≤r≤1.
