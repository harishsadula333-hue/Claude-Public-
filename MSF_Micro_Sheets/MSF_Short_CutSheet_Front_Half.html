Q. Define GCD of two integers a and b.
Ans: Let a, b be positive integers. The largest positive integer d which divides both a and b is called the greatest common divisor of a and b, i.e. gcd(a,b)=d. It is also known as Highest Common Factor (HCF). Ex: gcd(8,12)=4.

Q. Define Division algorithm.
Ans: If a, b∈Z and b>0, then there exist unique integers q and r such that a=bq+r, 0≤r<b. q is the quotient and r the remainder. Ex: 17=5×3+2.
UI·7
Q. Show that √2 is not a rational number.
Ans: Assume √2 is rational: √2=p/q, p,q positive integers, coprime.
Squaring: 2=p²/q² ⇒ p²=2q² ⇒ p² divisible by 2 ⇒ p divisible by 2. Let p=2m ⇒ 4m²=2q² ⇒ q²=2m² ⇒ q² divisible by 2 ⇒ q divisible by 2.
So p and q have common factor 2, contradicting our assumption. ∴ √2 is not a rational number.

Q. Define composite numbers.
Ans: A composite number is a natural number greater than 1 that has more than two factors, i.e. n=a·b with 1<a,b<n. Example: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12.

Q. Define Random variable and its types.
Ans: A real variable X whose value is determined by the outcome of a random experiment is called a random variable.
Types: (1) Discrete RV: takes countable values. Eg: no. of heads in 3 coin tosses. (2) Continuous RV: takes values over an interval. Eg: height, weight or temperature.

UII·9
Q. Define distribution function.
Ans: The distribution function or cumulative distribution function (CDF) of a random variable X is the probability that X takes a value less than or equal to x: F(x)=P(X≤x).
Properties: 1) 0≤F(x)≤1 2) F(x) is non-decreasing 3) F(−∞)=0 4) F(∞)=1 5) F(x) is right-continuous.

UIII·5
Q. Define Normal distribution.
Ans: Normal distribution is a continuous probability distribution that is symmetrical and bell-shaped around its mean. f(x)=[1/(σ√2π)]e^(−(x−μ)²/2σ²), −∞<x<∞.
Properties: 1) bell-shaped 2) mean=median=mode 3) symmetric about the mean 4) total area under the curve is 1 5) curve extends from −∞ to +∞.

Ans: Confidence interval for large samples: x̄ ± z_α/2 · σ/√n
x̄→sample mean, z_α/2→value for desired confidence level (1.96 for 95%, 2.58 for 99%), σ→standard deviation, n→sample size.

Q. Write about one-tailed test and two-tailed test.
Ans: One-tailed: if H₁ is one-tailed (right-tailed H₁: μ>μ₀ or left-tailed H₁: μ<μ₀), the test is one-tailed; the critical region lies entirely in one tail with area α. Critical z (5%)=1.645.
Two-tailed: H₀: μ=μ₀ vs H₁: μ≠μ₀; the critical region is split equally in both tails, area α/2 each. Critical z: 2.58 (1%), 1.96 (5%), 1.645 (10%).

UV·1
Q. Define correlation and types of correlations.
Ans: Correlation is a statistical analysis which measures and analyses the degree or extent to which two variables fluctuate with reference to each other; it expresses the relationship or interdependence of two sets of variables.
Types: 1) Positive and negative 2) Simple and multiple 3) Partial and total 4) Linear and non-linear.
UV·3
Q. Define Regression and types of Regressions.
Ans: The statistical method which helps us to estimate the unknown value of one variable from the known value of the related variable is called regression; the line describing the average relationship is the line of regression.
Types: simple and multiple; linear and non-linear; regression of y on x and of x on y.

Q. Methods of studying regressions.
Ans: Two methods: 1) Graphic method (scatter diagram, line of best fit drawn freehand) and 2) Algebraic method (regression equations y−ȳ=b_yx(x−x̄), x−x̄=b_xy(y−ȳ) using least squares/normal equations).

Q. Given that x=4y+5 and y=kx+4 are the regression lines of x on y and y on x respectively, show that 0≤k≤0.25.
Ans: x=4y+5 (x on y) ⇒ r·σx/σy=4; y=kx+4 (y on x) ⇒ r·σy/σx=k.
Multiplying: r²=4k. Since 0≤r²≤1 ⇒ 0≤4k≤1 ⇒ 0≤k≤1/4=0.25.

ρ=1−6[ΣD²+(1/12)(m³−m)+(1/12)(m³−m)+…]/(N³−N), m=no. of items with common rank.
