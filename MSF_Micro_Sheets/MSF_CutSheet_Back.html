Q. State and prove the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.
Ans: Statement: Every integer greater than 1 can be expressed as a product of prime numbers, and this factorisation is unique except for the order of the factors.
Proof:
Step 1: Existence | Let n>1 be an integer. | If 'n' is prime, its prime factorisation is 'n' itself. | If 'n' is composite, then n = a×b, 1<a<n, 1<b<n. | If 'a' & 'b' are composite, factorize them further. Continuing this process eventually expresses 'n' as a product of primes. | Thus, every integer greater than 1 has a prime factorisation.
Step 2: Uniqueness | Suppose an integer has two prime factorisations: n = p₁p₂…p_k = q₁q₂…q_m | By Euclid's lemma, if a prime divides a product, it must divide at least one of its factors. Therefore, p₁ must equal one of the primes q_j. Cancel this common prime factor from both sides. | Repeating this process shows that both factorisations contain exactly the same prime factors with the same multiplicities, possibly in a different order. | Hence, the prime factorisation is unique except for the order of the factors. Hence, proved.

1. The Null Hypothesis is H₀: x̄ = μ, i.e., "there is no significant difference between the sample mean and population mean" or the sample has been drawn from the parent population.
2. The Alternative Hypothesis is (i) H₁: x̄ ≠ μ (μ ≠ μ₀) or (ii) H₁: x̄ > μ (μ > μ₀) or (iii) H₁: x̄ < μ (μ < μ₀). Since n is large, the sampling distribution of x̄ is approximately normal.
3. Level of Significance. Set the level of significance α.
4. The Test Statistic: We have the following two cases.
Case I: When the standard deviation σ of population is known. In this case, standard error of mean, S.E.(x̄) = σ/√n, where n = sample size, σ = s.d. of population. ∴ The test statistic is z = (x̄ − μ)/S.E.(x̄) = (x̄ − μ)/(σ/√n).
Case II: When the standard deviation σ of population is not known. In this case, we take s, the s.d. of sample, to compute the S.E. of mean: S.E.(x̄) = s/√n. Hence the test statistic is z = (x̄ − μ)/S.E.(x̄) = (x̄ − μ)/(s/√n). | 5. Find the critical value z_α of z at the level of significance α from the normal table.
6. Decision: (a) If |z| < z_α, we accept the Null Hypothesis H₀. (b) If |z| > z_α, we reject the Null Hypothesis H₀. | The rejection rule for H₀: x̄ = μ (or μ = μ₀) — Table: Critical values of z (Level of significance α = 1% / 5% / 10%): | Critical region for μ≠μ₀: |z|>2.58 / |z|>1.96 / |z|>1.645; for μ>μ₀: z>2.33 / z>1.645 / z>1.28; for μ<μ₀: z<−2.33 / z<−1.645 / z<−1.28
